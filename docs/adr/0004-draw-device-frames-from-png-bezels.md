# 0004. Draw device frames from PNG bezels

- Status: accepted
- Date: 2026-10-25

## Context

The main reason for Handset is to show the phone inside a realistic device frame. Frames must be easy
to make and swap, and real manufacturer mockups are trademarked or copyrighted.

## Decision

A frame is an RGBA PNG whose screen area is fully transparent, plus a sidecar text file
`<name>.frame` holding `x y w h` of that area in pixels. The video is drawn first, the bezel on top, so
the bezel's rounded cut-out hides the video's square corners and no clipping shape is needed.

- Built-in frames (`obsidian`, `porcelain`, `hazel`) are generated by `frames/make_frames.py` and
  embedded in the binary. Users can pass their own PNG with `--frame`.
- Bezels are box-filtered down to at most 1800 px tall while decoding, with alpha-weighted
  averaging, so neither the decode buffer nor the GPU texture is larger than a full-height window needs.
- In landscape the bezel image is rotated 90 degrees clockwise and the screen area is remapped.
- A quarter-resolution alpha mask is kept for hit-testing, so only opaque bezel pixels act as a drag handle.

## Alternatives considered

- **Vector or procedurally drawn bezels.** Harder for users to bring their own.
- **A rounded-corner clip in the shader.** Works for a plain rounded rectangle but not for notches,
  punch-holes or buttons that overlap the screen.
- **Real device mockups.** Not redistributable.

## Consequences

- Any phone shape works if someone draws the PNG and measures the screen rectangle.
- A frame whose screen aspect ratio does not match the phone is letterboxed inside the screen area.
- Reverse-landscape (rotated the other way) is not handled yet.
